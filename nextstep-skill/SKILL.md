# NextStep Skill

## Purpose

Help a user who feels overwhelmed decide what to do **right now**.

The goal is not to produce a long productivity plan. The goal is to reduce cognitive load and give one clear next action.

## Trigger

Use when the user says things like:
- "I have too many things to do."
- "I don't know what to do first."
- "I'm anxious because everything feels urgent."
- "What should I do right now?"
- "Help me sort out my priorities."

## Safety check

Before prioritizing, scan for signs of imminent self-harm, violence, medical emergency, or inability to stay safe.

If present:
- do not continue ordinary prioritization;
- encourage immediate human / emergency support;
- keep the response focused on safety.

Otherwise continue normally.

## Step 1 — Extract open loops

List the user's concerns and tasks in neutral language.

Do not expand the list with new obligations.

## Step 2 — Classify each item

For each item, estimate:

- **Urgency**: Is there a near deadline?
- **Importance**: Does it materially affect the user's goals?
- **Controllability**: Can the user act on it now?
- **Effort**: Can meaningful progress happen in 5–30 minutes?
- **Dependency**: Is it waiting on someone else?
- **Reversibility**: Is delaying the decision costly or easy to undo?

## Step 3 — Park non-actionable items

Move items that depend mainly on other people, future events, or missing information into:

- Waiting
- Later
- Not actionable now

Do not keep them in the main priority list.

## Step 4 — Choose one next action

Prefer an action that is:

1. time-sensitive;
2. high-impact;
3. controllable;
4. small enough to start immediately.

If two tasks are close, prefer the one that reduces future uncertainty or unlocks other tasks.

## Step 5 — Return a short answer

Default format:

**Do now:** <one concrete action>

**Why:** <1–2 short reasons>

**Ignore for now:** <non-actionable items>

**Then:** <one next step after completion>

Avoid long motivational speeches, giant to-do lists, or ten-step plans.

## Good actions

Good:
- "Open the document and write the first 5 bullet points for tomorrow's meeting."
- "Reply to the email that has a deadline today."
- "Spend 20 minutes fixing the one reviewer comment blocking resubmission."

Bad:
- "Work on your PhD applications."
- "Be productive."
- "Solve your career problem."

The action should be physically startable within a few minutes.

## Tone

Calm, practical, non-patronizing.

Do not say:
- "take a breath"
- "calm down"
- "don't worry"

Do say:
- "The only thing you need to decide right now is..."
- "This can wait because..."
- "Start with..."

## Constraint

Do not optimize the user's entire life. Solve the next decision only.
